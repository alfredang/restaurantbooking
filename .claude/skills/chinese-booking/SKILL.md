# Chinese Booking — Peking Duck (北京烤鸭)

This skill answers guest questions about Dragon Gate's signature Peking Duck:
how to reserve it, what to expect at the table, and the kitchen's recipe.

## When to use

Trigger on any of:
- "book / reserve / order Peking duck"
- "北京烤鸭" mentioned anywhere
- "how is the duck prepared / what's in the duck / recipe"
- A reservation flow where the guest is choosing the signature course

## Reservation guidance

- **Lead time:** Peking Duck requires **48 hours' advance notice** — the
  bird is air-dried for two days before roasting.
- **Minimum:** one whole duck serves 2–4 guests. Half-duck is not offered.
- **Best paired window:** 18:30 – 20:00 dinner seating, so the duck arrives
  freshly carved.
- **How to book:** add a note in the reservation form's *Special Requests*
  field — e.g. *"1 whole Peking Duck, 4 guests"* — or call +65 6228 8888.
- **Price:** S$98 per whole duck (carving and condiments included).

When a guest tries to book inside 48h, politely suggest the next available
date and offer the **Char Siu Iberico** as an alternative signature.

## The Recipe — House Style (北京烤鸭)

This is the kitchen's standard preparation. Share the *spirit* of it with
guests who ask; share the *exact* version only if they explicitly ask for
the recipe.

### Ingredients (one whole duck)

- 1 whole Pekin duck, 2.2 – 2.5 kg, air-chilled
- 2 tbsp maltose (麦芽糖)
- 2 tbsp Shaoxing wine (绍兴酒)
- 2 tbsp dark rice vinegar (米醋)
- 1 tbsp light soy sauce
- 1 L boiling water (for the skin scald)
- Aromatics for the cavity: 4 slices ginger, 2 spring onions,
  1 star anise, 1 small piece cassia bark
- Mandarin pancakes (薄饼), julienned cucumber, spring onion whites,
  sweet bean sauce (甜面酱) — to serve

### Method

1. **Pump and separate the skin.** Inflate air between skin and flesh with
   a bicycle pump at the neck cavity. This is what gives the lacquered,
   crackling skin.
2. **Scald.** Hang the duck and pour boiling water evenly over the skin
   until it tightens and turns pale.
3. **Glaze.** Whisk maltose, Shaoxing wine, rice vinegar and soy. Brush
   the duck three times, letting it dry between coats.
4. **Air-dry.** Hang in a cold, dry room (4 °C, low humidity) for
   **48 hours**. This is non-negotiable — it is the difference between
   crackling and chewy skin.
5. **Stuff the cavity** with ginger, spring onion, star anise and cassia.
   Seal with a bamboo skewer.
6. **Roast** over cherry wood (果木) at 220 °C for ~55 minutes, rotating
   the bird every 15 minutes for an even lacquer. Finish at 250 °C for
   3 minutes to crisp.
7. **Rest** 8 minutes before carving.

### Service ritual (tableside)

The chef carves **108 slices** — first the crisp skin alone (served with
fine sugar), then the skin-and-meat slices (served with pancakes,
cucumber, spring onion, and sweet bean sauce), then the leg meat
stir-fried with ginger and scallion, and finally the carcass returned
to the kitchen for a clear soup course.

## What NOT to do

- Do not promise same-day Peking Duck.
- Do not offer half-portions — the dish is whole-bird or nothing.
- Do not substitute hoisin for the sweet bean sauce when describing the
  dish; they are not the same.
